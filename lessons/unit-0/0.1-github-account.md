# Lesson 0.1: Your GitHub Account and Practice Repo

**Unit 0: Groundwork** · About 1 hour

## Why this comes first

GitHub is where developers keep their code and work on it together. Everything in this course happens there:

- I'll send you lessons through GitHub.
- You'll hand in your work through GitHub.
- Later, the real WASSCE project will live there too.

In this lesson you'll make your GitHub account and your **practice repo**. A repo (short for *repository*) is a project folder that GitHub keeps track of. Your practice repo is where you'll do the exercises in this course. It's for practice, so mistakes there don't matter.

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- Sign in to GitHub with your password and a code from the authenticator app on your phone.
- Point to where your recovery codes are saved, including a copy on paper.
- Open your practice repo, `saikou-learning`, from its GitHub link.
- Find `narcisolobo` in your repo's list of collaborators.

## What you need

- Your laptop and an internet connection
- Your phone, for the security step
- An email address you check often

## Step 1: Choose a username

Your username is how other developers will know you. It appears on all your work, and you may put it on your CV one day. Choose carefully:

- Use your real name, or something close to it, for example your first and last name joined by a hyphen, or `saikoudev`.
- Keep it short and easy to spell.
- Avoid jokes and numbers like birth years.

You can change your username later, but it's easier not to have to.

## Step 2: Make your account

1. Go to [github.com/signup](https://github.com/signup).
2. Enter your email, a strong password and your username.
3. GitHub sends a code to your email. Enter it to confirm your account.
4. If GitHub asks questions about how you'll use it, you can answer or skip them. Choose the **Free** plan.

## Step 3: Turn on two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication (2FA) protects your account. When you sign in, GitHub asks for your password **and** a code from your phone. GitHub requires it for everyone who writes code, so let's set it up now.

1. Install an authenticator app on your phone, such as **Microsoft Authenticator** or **Google Authenticator**. Both are free.
2. On GitHub, click your profile picture (top right), then **Settings**.
3. Click **Password and authentication**, then **Enable two-factor authentication**.
4. Choose the authenticator app option. GitHub shows a QR code.
5. In the app on your phone, add an account and scan the QR code.
6. Type the 6-digit code from the app into GitHub.

**Save your recovery codes. This step is important.** GitHub gives you a list of recovery codes. If you lose your phone, they are the only way back into your account. Download them, and also write them on paper and keep it somewhere safe. Don't keep them only on your laptop.

## Step 4: Create your practice repo

1. Click the **+** at the top right of GitHub, then **New repository**.
2. **Repository name:** `saikou-learning`
3. **Description:** `My practice work while learning web development`
4. **Public or private:** this is your choice.
   - **Public:** anyone can see it. It shows your progress to future employers and collaborators. Your learning journal will be public too.
   - **Private:** only you and the people you invite can see it.

   I'd suggest public, but choose what you're comfortable with. You can change it later in the repo's settings.
5. Turn on **Add README**. This puts a first file in the repo. You'll need that in a later lesson.
6. Click **Create repository**.

## Step 5: Invite me as a collaborator

This lets me add lessons to your repo.

1. In your repo, click **Settings** (on the tabs at the top of the repo, not your account settings).
2. Click **Collaborators** in the menu on the left. GitHub may ask for your password or a 2FA code.
3. Click **Add people**, type `narcisolobo` and choose my account.
4. Click to add me. I'll get an email and accept.
